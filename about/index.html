Marigold is built in the open by a small group of people who think digital money forgot what cash got right. The software, the papers and this site are public on GitHub, the chain runs as a public testnet that anyone can join, and nothing about it is for sale.

What we do

Build the software, keep the lights on

We write and sign the wallet and the node, in the open, under an open-source licence. We run what a public testnet needs to be usable on day one: the public node that a fresh wallet connects to, an archival node that keeps the whole history, the faucet that hands out test notes, and the release channel that tells a wallet when a newer version exists. Every one of those is a convenience, not a dependency: a wallet can run its own node, anyone can run an archive, and the chain does not know we exist.

The company

marigold.cash Ltd

A company limited by guarantee, registered in England and Wales. Incorporation was applied for in October 2026; this page says so until the registration is confirmed, and then it will say the number.

A company limited by guarantee has no shareholders and pays no dividends. Its members guarantee a nominal sum if it is ever wound up, and that is the whole of their financial interest in it. The articles carry open-source clauses: the software the company publishes stays under an open-source licence, and the company cannot be turned into something that sells it.

What the company holds: the name, the domain, the keys that sign the releases, and the services listed above. What it does not hold: the coin. Nobody issues Marigold; it is mined, and the chain's rules decide who gets what. There is no token sale, no presale and nothing to buy, and if someone offers you Marigold for money before the network launches, they are not us.

The registrar

What the chain cannot check, someone has to

Almost everything on Marigold is checked by the chain itself: a note is real or it is not, a company's lane belongs to whoever claimed it first, a proof verifies or it does not. A few things are facts about the world that no chain can check, and for those the company acts as the registrar.

The first such thing is before our testers now: a proposal to pay shops that take Marigold at the till, a small share of the block reward drawn among verified tills, and a pot that a customer's payment at such a till can win. The chain can run both draws, but it cannot tell whether a shop exists. Checking that a business is real, giving its till its number and countersigning the claim that puts it on the chain is the registrar's work, and so is taking a till off the register when the shop closes or the device is stolen. The rules the registrar works by, how a till is verified, revoked and appealed, are written and published before the first till is registered, and the design is public while it is being discussed.

Company lanes need no registrar. The chain is the registry: the first valid claim of a tag holds it, and nobody keeps a list.
